Why the chair brakes the way it does

Two problems with the chair's braking, traced from the physics to the court logs from 11/22. Each section adds one piece. Read them in order the first time.

The story in six sentences

  1. When the chair brakes, the motors act as generators. The VESC decides how much current to pull out of them, and that current is the braking force.
  2. That current has to go somewhere, and it goes into the battery. We capped it at 3 A per side, so at speed the brakes are weak. (Problem 2)
  3. The VESC turns the battery cap into a motor-current limit by dividing by how fast the motor spins. Near a stop that division blows up, and the battery cap stops limiting anything.
  4. Our firmware tells the VESC to stop the wheel right now (setpoint 0 RPM) while it's still rolling at 1–2 m/s. The VESC then brakes as hard as its motor-current setting allows.
  5. The VESC's current control overshoots and oscillates, the current spikes past the VESC's ~50 A trip wire, and the VESC lets go of that wheel for 0.6 s. (Problem 1)
  6. Fix problem 1 by lowering the motor-current setting and commanding a smooth braking profile. Fix problem 2 by letting more braking current into the battery.

Every number on this site carries one of three labels:

measured read from the 11/22 court logs or the VESC settings. estimate calculated from measured numbers plus an assumption (usually the 120 kg chair-plus-rider mass). illustration a made-up shape that shows the idea, not our data.

Corrections to the first explainer (11/25 afternoon)

  • Motor voltage is about 6.6 V per m/s, not 7.9. The first number came from one log line. 6.6 is a fit across 2,329 VESC samples. measured
  • Push per amp is about 5 N per wheel, not 7.9. It comes from the VESC's own motor model, not from the voltage constant. estimate
  • The faults happened at 1–2 m/s, not 0.6 m/s. The first explainer's spike was a simulation. The real cause is the setpoint dropping to 0 while the wheel is still rolling (see "The 17:08 fault"). measured
  • The VESC's soft regen cutoff is already set: 55 V start, 57 V end. Nobody needs to add it. measured
