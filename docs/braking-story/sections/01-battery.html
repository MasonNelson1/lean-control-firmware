The battery

It says 48 V. We measure 53 V.

"48 V" is the battery's name, the class of system it fits into. It isn't a measurement. Our pack is a Bioenno BLF-4830A: 16 lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) cells in series. Each cell is officially 3.2 V, so the official pack voltage is 16 × 3.2 = 51.2 V. It's sold as "48 V" because it replaces a classic 48 V lead-acid pack (four 12 V batteries in series). A "12 V" car battery works the same way: it reads about 12.6 V at rest.

The voltage you actually measure depends on how full the pack is. LiFePO4 has a very flat middle. From about 20% to 90% charged, the pack sits around 52–53.8 V. On 11/22 we measured 53.4–53.6 V measured, so every calculation on this site uses 53.5 V.

Pack voltage at rest vs. how full it is
Typical curve for a 16-cell LiFePO4 pack, not Bioenno's own data estimate. Lines are the VESC settings read on 11/22 measured. Hover for values.

The VESCs already have four voltage settings that protect the battery and themselves. Both VESCs have the same values:

VESC settingValueWhat it does
l_max_vin57 VThe VESC's own hard limit. Above this it faults and lets go of the motor.
l_battery_regen_cut_end57 VBraking current into the battery is fully off by here.
l_battery_regen_cut_start55 VAbove this the VESC starts reducing braking current into the battery. This is the "dynamically back off braking when the voltage is high" feature, and it's already on.
l_battery_cut_start48 VBelow this the VESC starts reducing drive power (about 3.0 V per cell, nearly empty).
l_battery_cut_end44 VDrive power is fully off by here (2.75 V per cell).

Source: wheelchair-firmware/docs/vesc-config-2026-11-22.txt, read from both VESCs over CAN.

One thing to tighten: the regen cutoff ends at exactly 57 V, the same voltage where the VESC faults. Ending it at 56.5 V leaves a margin, so a fresh pack gets gentle braking instead of a fault. That's one VESC setting, and it needs a flagged upload.
