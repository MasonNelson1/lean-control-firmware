Overshoot

Why a sudden command overshoots and a smooth one doesn't

illustration This section is a teaching model, not the VESC. It's a generic springy controller, tuned so that jumping straight to −32.7 A crosses the trip wire, as it did on 11/22. Use it to see the shape of the idea, not to read off numbers.

Any feedback controller that reacts fast also tends to overshoot when its target jumps: it pushes hard to close the gap, can't stop exactly on the target, swings past, and rings back. How far it swings depends on two things you can control: how high the target is, and how suddenly it arrives.

Try the three buttons. Lowering the target to 20 A (fix 1) brings the peak well under the trip wire, even with an instant command. Ramping the command over a few hundred milliseconds nearly removes the overshoot, so the target can sit higher (more braking for Parker) and still clear the wire. That's why the full fix for problem 1 has two parts: the lower setting now, and a smooth braking command in the firmware.
