## Key findings
- **The plate is a seesaw.** It tips on the line through the side sensors, and the rider's centre of pressure sits ≈2" behind that line and never crosses it, so the front sensor reads ~0 at neutral.
- **The side sensors carry most of the information, forward/back included.** Left+right both up = forward; both down = back; opposite = turn (Charles's "two-bit" insight).
- **With tonight's gains (F7 B3 L.01 R.01), lean turning was effectively off,** forward hit full command a third of the way into the lean, and back couldn't reach full reverse. Centre-of-pressure / all-sensor math gives a smooth 0→full response in every direction and makes passive vs active neutral nearly identical.
- **Resolution is set by rider sway, not the math:** ≈10 steps forward, ≈6 back, ≈27 left/right. Back is limited physically; you can only lean back about half as far as forward.
- **Plate bolts made no difference. B2/B5/B6 back positions made no clear difference** once lean-to-lean variation was accounted for (B5 looked better at first, but that was Mason leaning deeper).
- **Foot position moves the rider's weight ≈1.5" forward** (feet hanging vs on the footrest). The footrest is easy to lower.
- **Both riders lean right more than left;** a sensor test shows left ≈ right, so it's real.
- **Known-weight tests: contact area dominates the reading.** Front/back gains are still unresolved.
