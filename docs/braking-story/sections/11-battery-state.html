Battery state

Full, normal, low: what voltage can tell us

LiFePO4's flat middle makes an exact percentage from voltage unreliable: 20% and 90% are only about 1.5 V apart, and driving at 20 A sags the voltage by about 1.6 V. But the two ends are steep and easy to read. That's exactly the three states that matter for safety:

StateResting voltageWhy it mattersProposed response
Full≥ 54.5 VJust off the charger. Braking pushes toward the VESC's 57 V fault. The VESC already backs off regen from 55 V.LED shows "braking limited" for the first minutes. Reset the gauge to 100%.
Normal50.5–54.5 VThe flat middle. Nothing to do.No LED.
Low48.5–50.5 VRoughly the last 10% estimate. Still safe, time to wrap up.One LED blinks slowly.
Empty< 48.5 VThe VESC starts cutting drive power at 48 V and stops at 44 V. Deep discharge shortens the battery's life.LED blinks fast. Stop using the chair and charge.

Reading resting voltage while driving. The Teensy already gets each VESC's battery voltage and current over CAN, 5–10 times a second. Correct for the sag with the pack resistance we measured from the logs (60–90 mΩ): resting ≈ measured + battery current × 0.08 Ω. Average it over a few seconds, and add a little hysteresis so the LED doesn't flicker between states. Blinking an LED is a behaviour change, so it gets flagged before upload. Which LED to use is still open.

A percentage gauge, if we want one later

Count charge instead of reading voltage. Each VESC already counts amp-hours used and regenerated (ah= in the logs). Remaining % ≈ 100% − net Ah used ÷ 30 Ah. Reset to 100% whenever the chair powers up with resting voltage ≥ 54.5 V (just charged). Store the running total in the Teensy's EEPROM, since the VESC counters reset at every power-up.

Don't route the charger through the VESCs. A VESC only measures current flowing between the battery and its own motor. There's no way to put the charger "behind" a VESC: the VESC has no charger input, and a charger connected to the battery bypasses it by design. The reset-to-100% rule gets the same information for free: after a full charge, the gauge knows it's at 100%.
