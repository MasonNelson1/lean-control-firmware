## 1. Findings, most severe first

**F1. Holding DISARM for half a second enters Assist, a powered mode.** [ModeStateMachine.cpp:87](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/ModeStateMachine.cpp:87), [:101](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/ModeStateMachine.cpp:101)
- **What happens:** a rider in LEAN or REMOTE hits DISARM in a panic and keeps holding it, which people do with a stop button. The chair brakes, then enters ASSIST. Once stopped, push-assist adds current in whatever direction the wheels are turned: a slope, a bump, another player.
- **How sure:** confirmed by simulation. After holding DISARM for 2.5 s the mode is ASSIST, and a 5 RPM nudge gets up to 4.8 A of boost. The existing test (`drive_controller_test.cpp` around line 350) treats this as intended.
- **Fix:** start the Assist hold only from a press that begins in DISARM: `assistHoldPending_ = selectedMode_ == DISARM && currentMode_ == DISARM;`. The press that stops the chair can then never become Assist. A different gesture would be better still.

**F2. Normal driving ignores the VESC's minimum ERPM, which creates a hidden dead zone and a jump.** [DriveController.cpp](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/DriveController.cpp) `driveRPM`, against [drive_constants.h:31](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/drive_constants.h:31)
- The firmware's own comment (and the test `Sim`) says speed mode lets go of the motor below about 15.5 wheel RPM, which is 0.49 m/s. The stop code works around this; the drive code doesn't.
- Using the test model, the first lean that produces a wheel command above that threshold is:

  | Speed-knob position | Lean needed (share of range) |
  |---|---|
  | 1 | 58 % |
  | 2 | 40 % |
  | 3 | 32 % |
  | 4 | 27 % |

- Below that the motor does nothing; above it the chair steps straight to about 0.5 m/s. That is "nothing, nothing, then everything".
- **Turning on the spot:**
  - Nothing happens until 72 % of sideways lean, then the chair snaps to about 94 deg/s.
  - When the rider straightens up, the motors let go below about 94 deg/s and the chair coasts in rotation. That undermines the 900 deg/s² turn-decel fix for overshoot.
- **Response time (Spec K):** the first command a VESC would act on comes 250 ms after a full lean (160 ms for a spin), because of the jerk limit plus this threshold.
- **How sure:** the drive-path behaviour is confirmed by simulation. The VESC behaviour itself is the firmware's own claim; I couldn't check it, because `vesc-reference/` has only the hardware config.
- **Fix:** mostly on the bench. Lower `s_pid_min_erpm` (for example to 150–300 ERPM, about 3–5 wheel RPM), check that low-speed tracking is acceptable with hall sensors, then make `STOP_HANDOFF_RPM` follow it. Add a test that drives at half lean.

**F3. Steering is mirrored while braking with a back lean (this was already in the old firmware).** [LeanDriveMode.cpp:111-113](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/driveModes/LeanDriveMode.cpp:111)
- The reverse-turn flip is keyed to the lean command, not to the chair's direction of travel.
- **What happens:** at 2.46 m/s the rider leans back to brake and leans right. The chair turns left, at up to 69 deg/s, while still moving forward from 2.1 m/s down to 0.5 m/s. A full back lean while moving forward gives no steering at all.
- **How sure:** confirmed by simulation.
- **Fix:** keep the team's −0.3 / −0.9 numbers, but blend on the direction of travel, using `motion_.linearRPM()` (or measured speed) normalised to the knob limit, instead of the lean.

**F4. Turning at the speed limit gets around the acceleration limit.** [MotionSmoother.h:74-80](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/motion/MotionSmoother.h:74)
- The "shift both wheels" step runs after the linear smoother, so the smoother never sees the change.
- **What happens:**
  - Adding a full turn at the knob limit brakes the chair at 3.1 m/s².
  - Straightening up re-accelerates it at 4.7 m/s². That's above `MAX_ACCEL` (3.7) on every knob position.
  - On knob 1, a full turn at full speed drops the chair from 1.23 m/s to 0.39 m/s, and the inner wheel runs backwards.
- **How sure:** confirmed by simulation.
- **Fix:** cap the linear target before smoothing, at `maxRPM − |turn_|`, so the linear smoother applies its own limits. Keep the shift only as a final guard.

**F5. The ±50 RPM clamp lets the smoother wind up, so the chair lurches after a stall.** [DriveController.cpp:205](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/DriveController.cpp:205)
- **What happens:** the wheels are held under a full lean (curb, mat edge, a helper's hands). The smoother keeps climbing to the target speed while the command sits 50 RPM above the wheel. When the wheels come free, the command stays about 48 RPM ahead of the wheel, so the VESC accelerates at its current limit instead of the design rate. Free-wheeling, the command is never more than about 15 RPM ahead.
- **How sure:** confirmed by simulation.
- **Fix:** when the clamp is active, pull the smoother's state back to the clamped values (a clip and an acceleration reset in `RPMSetpointPID`).

**F6. The remote starts driving on its first packet without requiring a centred stick.** [RemoteDriveMode.cpp:26, 59](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/driveModes/RemoteDriveMode.cpp:26)
- This applies when REMOTE is selected, when the link comes back after a timeout, and at boot if `defaultMode` is REMOTE.
- **What happens:** after a 3 s dropout with the stick held forward, the chair is commanded to 72 RPM within a second of the link returning.
- **How sure:** confirmed by simulation.
- **Fix:** after `reset()` or a timeout, reject non-zero drive packets until one arrives with both axes near zero (about 0.05).

**F7. Profiles carry the VESC IDs and the wheel circumference.** [DriveSettings.cpp:168-172](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/config/DriveSettings.cpp:168)
- These fields aren't marked `calibration`, so loading a profile overwrites them.
- **What happens:** a profile saved while the IDs were swapped loads swapped IDs. The cross-check only requires the two IDs to differ, so it passes, and steering is mirrored in both LEAN and REMOTE.
- **How sure:** confirmed by reading.
- **Fix:** mark `leftVesc`, `rightVesc` and `wheelCircumference` as calibration (chair-owned) fields.

**F8. A tare isn't refused when someone is seated.** [DriveController.cpp:251-253](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/DriveController.cpp:251)
- **What happens:** holding calibrate for 2 s in DISARM with a rider aboard sets the zeros to that rider's readings (F505 B430 L401 R481). The chair then reads the rider as not seated and won't arm. That fails safe, but the cause isn't visible.
- A tare with a partial load (a hand, a bag) raises the zeros instead. That can cause seat-empty stops in the middle of a ride.
- **How sure:** confirmed by simulation.
- **Fix:** require `!lean_.isSeated()` to start a tare and abort it if the seat becomes occupied.

**F9. The ADC-rail fault can't detect an unplugged sensor, though its message says it can.** [DriveController.cpp:74](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/DriveController.cpp:74)
- The sensors are 2-wire FSRs, and an unloaded FSR is effectively an open circuit. So an unplugged sensor reads about 372, the same as an unloaded one.
- **What happens:** if a back-sensor wire breaks during a reverse lean, the reading looks like a forward lean.
- **How sure:** confirmed by reading the board files (J6–J9 are 2-pin GND plus signal) together with the 372-count fact.
- **Fix:** correct the message. Optionally, add a plausibility fault: one sensor sitting at its zero for several seconds while the rider is seated and the opposite sensor is heavily loaded.

**F10. The dashboard and calibration bounds allow unsafe values.**
- `KneeBounds` at [DriveSettings.cpp:39](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/config/DriveSettings.cpp:39) allows an exponent near 0.0001, which makes the response on/off: any lean past the deadband means full speed. Require knee output ≤ knee input, or bound the exponent to roughly 1–4.
- `maxLateralAccel` goes up to 15 m/s², which is 1.5 g. Even the default 4 m/s² (0.41 g) isn't far from the tip-over threshold of a narrow-track chair (about 0.5 g). I'd use 2 m/s² for first rides and cap the setting at about 5.
- The range calibration accepts any direction that reached 40 counts, against about 730 for a real rider. A half-done calibration makes that direction a hair trigger. Accept only about 150 counts or more.
- **How sure:** confirmed by reading.

**F11. A watchdog reset at speed ends in coasting.** [main.cpp:206](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/main.cpp:206)
- The VESCs hold the last speed command until their own timeout (1 s by default). The firmware then boots in DISARM and sends zero current, with no stop.
- **How sure:** confirmed by reading.
- **Fix:** if the watchdog caused the reset and the wheels report speed, run a stop based on the measured speed.

*Confirmed by simulation. High.*

Where: [DriveController.cpp:243](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/DriveController.cpp:243). Normal LEAN and REMOTE driving only ever sends RPM commands. `keepBraking` holds the command at 18 RPM only while the wheel is above 22 RPM. Below that, the command drops into the VESC's dead zone (under 15.5 RPM by default), the VESC lets go, and the wheel freewheels. What the simulation shows:

*Confirmed by simulation. High. This is the team's design, but as coded I think it is unsafe.*

Where: [PushAssist.h:31](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/driveModes/PushAssist.h:31). Boost depends on wheel speed alone, and the curve has exponent 0.6. That is positive feedback. The fade at 85% of the knob limit only caps where it settles.

**Smallest fix:**

## 1. Findings, most severe first

### Real defects

**F1. After every stop the motors are released, and nothing brakes the chair again. On a slope it rolls away, and if the seat is empty it rolls away with nobody in it.** Confirmed by simulation.
- **Where:** `src/motion/StopController.h:121-122` ends the brake phase on the 1.5 s timeout or the 150 ms settle, then returns Release. `src/DriveController.cpp:275-276` sends zero current whenever no stop is running, and `:579` sends DISARM through that path.
- **What happens:** The rider gets out on a 1:12 ramp while in LEAN. The chair brakes to under 3 RPM, releases at about 0.8 s, and the empty chair then speeds up to 4.1 m/s after 8 s. Parked in DISARM on the same ramp, it reaches 2.8 m/s in 5 s.
- **Same gap in other states:** a VescSilent fault (blind brake for 1.5 s, then free), Assist, LEAN while not yet calibrated, and REMOTE waiting for a centred stick. All of these send `release()` whatever the wheels are doing.
- **Smallest fix:** in every state that isn't driving, send brake current whenever a fresh wheel speed is above about 3 RPM. Brake current can never drive. If feedback is gone, keep brake current on rather than timing out.
- **Trade-off for the team:** pushing the chair by hand in DISARM would be resisted. Assist exists for that, or add an explicit free-wheel condition. The default should not be a free roll.

**F2. A back lean to brake turns straight into reverse at the full speed-knob limit. Reverse has no cap of its own.** Confirmed by simulation.
- **Where:** `src/DriveController.cpp:529` (`command.speed * speedLimitMS_`, where speed can be -1) and `:549-552` for REMOTE.
- **What happens:** On knob 2 at 2.46 m/s, a full back lean held down stops the chair at about 0.85 s. Without pausing it accelerates backwards and reaches -2.46 m/s by 1.5 s. On knob 4 that becomes -4.9 m/s, backwards, with no rear view.
- **Why it matters:** "lean back to stop" is the panic gesture, and a startle reflex is to lean back and hold it.
- **Smallest fix:** cap reverse at a fraction of the knob limit (one constant, applied in both modes). Add a latch: once a back lean has brought forward travel to rest, hold zero until the lean comes back into the deadband before reverse is allowed.

**F3. REMOTE has no turn-rate or sideways-acceleration limit.** Confirmed by simulation.
- **Where:** `src/DriveController.cpp:544-553`. The `maxTurnRate` / `maxLateralAccel` cap at `:534-540` is applied only in LEAN.

| 3 | 352 deg/s | 11.3 m/s² |

**F6. A lost settings bank falls back to defaults without saying so.** Confirmed by reading, minor.

## 1. Findings, most severe first

### Real defects

**F1. A stop can drive the motors forward for about a second.**
- **Where:** [DriveController.cpp:199-213](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/DriveController.cpp:199) and the ramp in [StopController.h:85-136](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/motion/StopController.h:85).
- **What goes wrong:** the stop ramp is open-loop. It is only clamped to within 50 RPM of each wheel, in either direction. If the wheels slow faster than the ramp, the VESC is commanded above wheel speed and pushes.
- **Scenario:** DISARM at top speed on knob 4, wheels blocked 150 ms later (the chair hits a wall, a person catches it, or the rider clamps the rims). The firmware sends +50 RPM to stalled wheels for about 0.7 s, then follows the ramp down. That is 1065 ms of forward speed commands in total. The same happens after a seat-empty stop on knob 2 (530 ms), which is the case where the rider has fallen out in front of the chair.
- **Confidence:** confirmed by running.
- **Why it slipped through:** normal driving has `MotionSmoother::unwind()` for exactly this; the stop path has no equivalent. `expectStop()` only checks that commands fall, never that they stay at or below the wheels.
- **Smallest fix:** while feedback is fresh, cap each ramp command at that wheel's measured speed (same sign, never past zero), using the same `start()` rule `beginStop` already applies on the first tick. Go to brake current once the capped command is under the handoff speed. Add a blocked-wheel test.

**F2. A stop that has not reached rest in 5 s releases the motors.**
- **Where:** [StopController.h:153-155](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/motion/StopController.h:153), `STOP_BRAKE_LIMIT_MS` at [drive_constants.h:88](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/drive_constants.h:88).
- **Scenario:** the rider presses DISARM while descending a slope steeper than the brake current can hold. The ramp runs, then 15 A brake for 5 s, then zero current, because DISARM with a rider aboard does not hold. In my run the stop "ended" at 5.1 s with the wheels at 71 RPM, and three seconds later they were at 239 RPM.
- **Confidence:** confirmed by running; how steep a slope triggers it depends on F3.
- **Smallest fix:** a stop that times out with the wheels still turning should behave like `faultHold_`: keep braking until DISARM is pressed again or a mode is armed. Brake current cannot drive, so holding it is safe.

**F3. The tests overstate low-speed braking, so the hold and turn-stop claims are not evidence.**
- **Where:** the plant model at [drive_controller_test.cpp:56](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/tests/native/drive_controller_test.cpp:56); it affects the assertions at lines 1440, 1479, 1482 and 1499-1625.
- **What the VESC does:** in brake-current mode it shorts the phases whenever reaching the set current would need reverse voltage (`mcpwm_foc.c:3351-3366`). At low speed the real brake current is therefore back-EMF over resistance, well under the 15 A asked for. The sim gives full brake force at any speed (15 A = 100 RPM/s).
- **Consequences:**
  - The hold in `parked()` and the dead-zone braking in `driveRPM` are proportional only (1 A per RPM), so the chair creeps on any slope. With a brake strength of 50 RPM/s at 15 A (my estimate from the CDR motor table), a chair in LEAN at neutral on a 1-in-12 ramp rolls at 7.6 RPM, 4.7 m in 20 s. On 1-in-6 it rolls at 22 RPM.
  - This is the same with the VESC minimum lowered: a zero command always takes the brake-current branch, never the speed loop.
  - The end of every pivot turn is passive braking from 22 RPM (133 deg/s) with VESC defaults. The "under 30 degrees" assertion comes from the optimistic model.
- **Confidence:** VESC mechanism confirmed by reading; the creep figures are simulated with my estimated brake constant. Real magnitudes depend on motor resistance, which I do not have.
- **Smallest fix:**
  1. Make the sim's brake current speed-limited and re-run.
  2. Add an integral term to the hold current (safe, since it cannot drive).
  3. Let `vescMinSpeed` go to 0, so a VESC set up for zero-speed hold can be given an RPM 0 command ([DriveSettings.cpp:192-194](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/config/DriveSettings.cpp:192) floors it at 1.0).

**F4. A stop that begins while the wheels lag a falling command skips the speed loop.**
- **Where:** [DriveController.cpp:126-129](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/DriveController.cpp:126).
- **What goes wrong:** `beginStop` starts from the smaller of command and wheel. If the command is already under the handoff speed, it goes straight to 15 A brake current whatever the wheel speed.
- **Scenario:** heavy chair, sit up from top speed, press DISARM late. The wheel was at 62.7 RPM being braked by the speed loop (RPM 18), and DISARM switched it to BRK 15 A.

- **Smallest fix:** when a wheel is above handoff, start the ramp at the handoff speed in the wheel's direction, so the existing "hold until the wheels come down" logic runs.

**F5. One bad speed reading raises the other wheel's command above the knob limit.**
- **Where:** [DriveController.cpp:319-323](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/DriveController.cpp:319).
- **Scenario:** knob 1 (limit 38.8 RPM), left reading jumps to 200 for one tick. Commands that tick were left 150, right 88.8. It recovers on the next tick.
- **Confidence:** confirmed by running; needs a VESC estimator glitch, so low.

**F6. Assist takes a short gravity roll as a push.**

## What changed and why

**Safety**
- **Stops:** every loss of drive now brakes (speed ramp, then brake current) instead of coasting. A stop never commands above wheel speed.
- **Holding:** a chair nothing is driving is held with brake current. It is not held in Assist, or in DISARM with a rider seated. After a fault it stays held until DISARM is pressed.
- **Faults:** a silent VESC (80 ms), a sensor at an ADC rail, and the remote's stop button all disarm, in every mode. A broken sensor wire can no longer create motion.
- **Watchdog and boot:** 0.5 s watchdog; the chair always powers on in DISARM and brakes if it is rolling.
- **Seat check:** one unit, a fixed threshold, hysteresis and hold times.
- **DISARM:** always stops, however long it is held; it can no longer slide into Assist.

**Control and turning**
- **Lean math:** each direction is the load gained on that sensor pair, divided by a per-direction range. I did not use centre of pressure: the log shows total load more than doubles in a lean, which would turn a forward lean into a turn.
- **Turning:** a rate (160 deg/s at full lean) on every speed-knob position, smoothed separately from forward speed, capped at speed by sideways acceleration.
- **Sitting up:** stops the chair. Wheels inside the VESC's minimum-speed zone get brake current, and the command can no longer run ahead of the wheels.
- **Reverse:** half speed. A back lean that starts while rolling forward only brakes.
- **Reverse-turn threshold:** −0.3 is still the centre, but it fades instead of stepping, and it no longer applies while the chair still rolls forward (it steered the wrong way under braking).
- **REMOTE:** goes through the same turn and reverse limits, and needs a centred stick first.

**Calibration**
- **Neutral:** averaged over a still second after the button is released, not a 40 ms snapshot during the press.
- **Range calibration:** hold 1 s; replaces dynamic calibration, is bounded per direction, and is saved.
- **Tare:** in DISARM with the seat empty, hold 2 s; measures sensor zeros.
- **Persistence:** both survive power cycles and profile loads.
- **Drift:** neutral slowly follows sensor drift at rest, bounded at 15%.

**Tuning**
- **Removed:** per-sensor gains, the turn-mix curve, calibration sample count, the shared floor and the startup-mode setting.
- **Added:** four ranges, four zeros, seat threshold, turn rate, sideways limit, track width and VESC minimum speed.
- **Dashboard:** edits apply immediately. It shows the ranges and a health badge; this part was built by an Opus 5.5 subagent and I reviewed the diff.

**Other**
- **Assist:** redesigned so it cannot drive itself. A boost must be earned by a push and fades in 0.7 s.
- **Structure:** `DriveController` holds the logic with no hardware dependency, so whole rides run on the host, including 270 randomized rides checked against safety rules every tick.
- **VESC reference:** I saved three upstream VESC source files to `~/LEAN/vesc-firmware-ref/` (outside the repo).

## What reviewers kept flagging that I didn't resolve

- **VESC minimum-speed dead zone** (every round): only fixable on the bench. The firmware works around it for stopping and logs a hint.
- **`main.cpp` has no host tests** (every round): it isn't host-compiled; fixing that means moving the profile and settings-gate logic out.
- **Spec B** (most rounds): the speed command ends 0.23, 0.56, 0.89 and 1.22 s after a stop begins, by knob position. Meeting 0.5 s from top speed needs 8.7 m/s², more than the motors can deliver.
- **LEAN needs a fresh calibration on every entry**, even after a quick DISARM. I kept it: it is your design and it fails safe.
- **One EEPROM bank:** a power cut mid-save resets settings. It is now logged at boot, but a second bank doesn't fit beside seven profiles.
- **Braking out of reverse** un-flips the turn at once. Low severity; left alone.
- **Disagreed with:**
  - acting on DISARM before debounce;
  - an integral term on the hold current;
  - scaling turn rate with the speed knob.
