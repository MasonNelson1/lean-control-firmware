## Firmware review at `bac4d59`: 7/10

The firmware is careful and mostly sound, but I found one real safety defect. After a startled lean back and to one side, the chair stops and then spins in place, in the direction opposite to the lean, until the rider sits up. I confirmed it in simulation with stock VESC settings.

All host tests pass (12 native suites, 239 dashboard JS, 74 Python) and `pio run` builds for teensy41. My scratch simulations are in `/tmp/lean-review-opus/` (s1–s6). I modified no repository files and made no commits.

### Findings, most severe first

**1. A held back-and-sideways lean spins the chair the wrong way after it stops.** Real defect, confirmed by running.
- **Where:** `DriveController.cpp:473–498` and `LeanDriveMode.cpp:126–128`.
- **How it goes wrong:** At speed, the rider leans back and to the right and holds it (the startled case the code was written for).
  - While the chair brakes, it turns right, which is correct.
  - Below 15% of the speed limit, the reverse-turn flip fades in. Reverse is locked, so the speed command stays 0, but the turn has already been flipped. The chair is sent a pure in-place pivot to the left.
- **Measured (s1, s4):**
  - Lean back 300 counts (75% of the back range) and right 450 (90% of the right range), with stock min ERPM: the chair turns **490° left in 5 s, still spinning at 135 deg/s**.
  - Smaller side leans fall under the VESC's 15.5 RPM minimum, so nothing happens today. Once the team lowers `s_pid_min_erpm`, as the firmware tells them to, a 60% side lean already pivots at 69 deg/s.
- **Why the tests miss it:** The test at `drive_controller_test.cpp:1022–1045` only asserts on simulated wheel speeds. Its simulated VESC ignores commands under 15.5 RPM, so the ±11.5 RPM pivot commands sent at standstill never show up.
- **Smallest fix:** Set or clear `reverseLocked_` before calling `lean_.command()`. While it is locked, call `command(1.0f)` so the flip and the hard-reverse cutoff never apply. The other option is to zero the turn while locked with a negative speed.
  - Add a test that asserts on `sim.last` commands rather than wheel speeds, or one that sets `vescMinRPM` low.

**2. When the rider sits up mid-acceleration, the chair keeps speeding up for about 0.25 s.** Real for a VESC that tracks its speed command tightly. Confirmed in simulation; the size depends on how far the real VESC lags its command.
- **Where:** `rpm_setpoint_pid.h:134–135`.
- **How it goes wrong:** The jerk limit (500 RPM/s², about 15.8 m/s³) also limits *ending* an acceleration. Going from 3.7 m/s² of acceleration to zero takes 0.23 s. `unwind()` only steps in when the command leads the wheel by more than 5 RPM.
- **Measured (s3, s6):**
  - With a tight-tracking simulated VESC, sitting up 0.2–0.5 s into a full lean adds **+0.47–0.54 m/s**. One case went from 0.64 to 1.14 m/s, with the chair still speeding up for 250 ms after the rider sat up.
  - With a laggier VESC, `unwind()` catches it and the extra speed falls to about 0.05 m/s.
- **Why it matters:** This is complaint 1 (overshoot) on the forward axis, which this branch fixed only for turning.
- **Smallest fix:** In `smoothRPM`, set `acceleration_` to zero whenever it has the opposite sign to `error`, before the jerk step. Stopping a speed-up is allowed to be sudden. Building up braking stays jerk-limited.

**3. Braking depends on VESC "allow braking", and nothing in the firmware flags it or detects it.** Missing safeguard plus a gap in the bench notes. Confirmed by running, against a model.
- **Where:** `StopController.h:99–100`, and every RPM-mode slow-down.
- **How it goes wrong:** Both normal braking and the stop ramp are RPM commands that fall faster than the wheel. If `s_pid_allow_braking` is off, the VESC coasts. The stop ramp then holds its floor for up to another second waiting for wheels that will never follow.
- **Measured (s5):** With braking disabled in the simulated VESC:
  - A DISARM stop from top speed takes 3.73 s, which misses Spec P (3.0 s).
  - Sitting up at speed in LEAN never stops the chair. It only coasts.
- **Context:** The VESC default is "on", and the brief lists this setting as bench-only. But unlike min ERPM and the app timeout, no comment mentions it.
- **Fix:**
  - Add it to the bench checklist in `drive_constants.h`.
  - During a stop ramp, switch to brake current as soon as a wheel is more than about 10 RPM above the ramp, instead of waiting out `STOP_RAMP_OVERRUN_MS`.
  - Optionally, log a hint the way `watchLowSpeed` does.

**4. A firmware hang leaves the last speed command running for about 1 s.** Partly flagged in comments. Confirmed by reading.
- **Where:** `main.cpp:211` and `watchdog.h`.
- **How it goes wrong:** The exposure is the 0.5 s watchdog plus Teensy boot plus `setup()`, which includes a `delay(200)` that serves no purpose (boot recovery already waits for VESC frames). With stock VESC settings (1 s timeout, timeout brake current 0), the chair carries on at the last speed command and then coasts.
- **Why it matters:** Spec B can't be met for a hang unless the VESCs are configured for it.
- **Fix:**
  - Remove the `delay(200)`.
  - In `watchdog.h`, state target values: VESC timeout of about 200 ms and a non-zero timeout brake current.

**5. Matters of taste and minor points**
- **CAL bumped mid-ride stops the chair hard.** It triggers a 3.7 m/s² stop (`DriveController.cpp:406`). That is safe in direction, but surprising for the rider.
  - I checked whether the neutral captured afterwards could make sitting up command reverse. The rule that ignores lost load keeps that under about 3% (s2).
  - I'd ignore CAL while the chair is moving.
- **Profile LED after Revert/Defaults.** It keeps showing the old profile even though that profile's values are gone.
- **Jerk unit label.** `rpm_setpoint_pid.h:23` labels the jerk "RPM/s²". The quantity is the rate of change of acceleration, which is RPM/s³.
- **Turning on knob position 1.** A full side lean while driving forward takes 26.5 of the 38.8 RPM limit for the turn, so the inner wheel runs backwards. This is intended, but worth feeling on the bench.

### What I checked and found sound
- **Faults:** All three fault types and their timing. Faults are raised before mode logic, so LEAN, REMOTE and ASSIST never run without fresh VESC feedback. The hold after a fault lasts until DISARM is pressed. Boot recovery covers both the silent-VESC and rolling cases.
- **Stops:** The two-phase stop controller, the start-from-wheel logic, and the envelope clamp.
- **Low-speed handling:** The handoff from RPM commands to brake current matches bldc's `foc_math.c:543` and the duty-0 brake in `mcpwm_foc.c:3373`.
- **Holding when not driven:** `parked()` hold and free-wheel rules.
- **Mode state machine:** Interrupt edge plus debounced press, DISARM working with an empty seat and during calibration, the Assist hold guard, profile reservation and cancel, and Assist not re-entering when the seat flickers.
- **Lean input:** Lost load is ignored, two-wire sensor failures can't create motion, the deadband and curve are bounded (exponent 1–4), and trim is bounded.
- **Tare and calibration:** Tare is refused when seated, and neutral is reset whenever LEAN is re-entered (complaint 3).
- **Remote:** Recentre after a timeout or soft stop, and the timeout bounds.
- **Settings and EEPROM:** Settings are blocked while not disarmed or still stopping. The layout fits: 4153 of 4284 bytes. Profiles can't change calibration or VESC IDs.
- **Other:** Millis rollover in every `now - x` comparison, the CAN encodings against `comm_can.c`, the 19-column telemetry, and the knob debounce and fault handling.

### Questions I could not settle
1. What does a hard lean peak at in raw counts? If any amplifier gets near 4000, the rail fault will stop the chair mid-ride.
2. Braking at 3.7 m/s² pitches the rider forward, which loads the front sensor and adds forward command, so the stop works against itself. The run2 log has no wheel speeds, so I couldn't check how strong this is for an upper-body-only rider. It needs a wheels-on test.
3. Are the motors sensorless? That decides how far `s_pid_min_erpm` can really be lowered, and so whether complaint 2 ("nothing, nothing, then everything") is fixable at all.
4. Can a bump drop the seat load under 120 counts for 100 ms? If so, it ends the ride with a hard stop and forces a recalibration.

### Score: 7/10
What holds it down is finding 1: a startled rider can get a sustained wrong-way spin, and the test meant to cover it can't see it. Findings 2 and 3 are behaviour the rider would notice on the bench.

- Stop acceleration as soon as the rider asks for less speed.
- Make stops brake even when the VESC's speed loop won't, and give target VESC timeout and allow-braking settings in the code.

Everything else is ready for wheels-off testing.
