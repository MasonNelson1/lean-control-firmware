## 1. Findings, most severe first

**F1. The remote's e-stop is ignored in LEAN and ASSIST. Real defect, safety.**
- **Where:** the soft-stop latch is set in [RemoteDriveMode.cpp:45](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/driveModes/RemoteDriveMode.cpp:45), but only the REMOTE case reads it ([DriveController.cpp:558](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/DriveController.cpp:558)).
- **What goes wrong:** the remote's button is named `estop` in its own code, and it sends a soft stop 100 times a second "no matter what if we are connected" (`controller-firmware/src/main.cpp`). In supervised LEAN riding, the spotter holding the remote is the obvious person to stop the chair, and pressing that button does nothing. The latch also stays set, so the next time someone selects REMOTE it is already stopped.
- **How sure:** I ran it. LEAN at 77.5 RPM, then one second of soft-stop packets: still LEAN, still 77.7 RPM.
- **Smallest fix:** add a `softStopLatched()` getter to RemoteDriveMode. In `tick()`, whenever a mode is selected and the latch is set, call `requestDisarm()` and set `faultHold_ = true`; refuse to arm any mode while it is latched; log it. Add one test each for LEAN and ASSIST.
- If the team really doesn't want the remote to stop LEAN, the remote and the riding procedure have to say so plainly. I would not ride with it as it is.

**F2. Selecting REMOTE while rolling faster than the speed-knob limit steps the speed command down at once. Real, minor.**
- **Where:** the `!driving_` branch in [DriveController.cpp:312](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/DriveController.cpp:312) sets the smoother to the wheel speed, then [MotionSmoother.h:56](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/motion/MotionSmoother.h:56) and the clip in `smoothRPM` cut it straight to the limit.
- **What goes wrong:** I simulated knob position 1 (limit 38.8 RPM), the chair rolling at 65 RPM, REMOTE selected with the stick centred. The first command is 38.8 RPM, a 0.8 m/s drop in one tick, not the 3.7 m/s² ramp. The VESC then brakes as hard as its speed loop allows. `slewDown` ([DriveController.cpp:430](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/DriveController.cpp:430)) exists to prevent exactly this step, but only when the knob is turned down.
- LEAN is not affected, because LEAN always arms through a calibration stop.
- **Fix:** when engaging, raise `speedLimitMS_` to at least the measured forward speed (`|measuredLinear| / LINEAR_TO_RPM`), so `slewDown` brings it down at MAX_ACCEL.

**F3. Every entry into LEAN throws away the calibrated neutral, even if the rider never left the seat. Matter of taste, but it costs usability.**
- **Where:** [ModeStateMachine.cpp:134](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/ModeStateMachine.cpp:134) returns `resetLean = (next == LEAN)` on every transition into LEAN, and [DriveController.cpp:174](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/DriveController.cpp:174) resets the lean when it's set.
- **Effect:** tap DISARM at a doorway, tap LEAN, and the chair won't move until the rider presses calibrate and sits still for about 1.3 s.
- **Seat bounces cause it too.** With `SeatOffS = 0.08` ([LeanDriveMode.h:85](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/driveModes/LeanDriveMode.h:85)), a 100 ms seat dip at 3.7 m/s triggered a full 0.38 g stop in my sim, and the chair then sat uncalibrated.
- It fails safe, and it is the fix for complaint 3. But it only needs to happen when the seat was actually empty for a while (say over 1 s), after a tare, or after a profile load. Otherwise riders will learn to resent the button.

**F4. The glue code in `main.cpp` has no tests. Test gap.**
- Untested pieces:
  - profile confirmation end to end (knob → pending → hold LEAN → activate);
  - the "rolling or stopping" gate on settings writes ([main.cpp:337–339](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/main.cpp:337));
  - the `dt` cap;
  - the knob-to-speed-limit path.
- They read correctly. This is just where a regression would go unnoticed.

**Nits**

- A dashboard Revert reloads the saved settings bank over the active profile, while the profile LED still shows that profile.

## 2. What I checked and found sound
- **Pins:** the sensor, button and LED pins in `common.h` match the PCB README (J6=A17 back, J7=A16 right, J8=A15 front, J9=A14 left).
- **VESC CAN:**
  - Frame IDs, scaling, big-endian packing and STATUS_1 decoding are correct.
  - Brake current never drives a wheel, and it shorts the phases near a standstill.
  - Below the minimum ERPM, current VESC firmware brakes the motor on duty 0.
  - When speed mode starts or comes back, the VESC's speed setpoint starts from the measured speed.
  - The firmware's handoff from speed commands to brake current is consistent with all of the above.
- **Stops:**
  - Stop ramp arithmetic: 117 RPM/s, giving 0.23 / 0.56 / 0.89 / 1.22 s by knob position, as documented.
  - Spec P is met. Spec B is honestly flagged as a team decision, and 8.7 m/s² to meet it is not a sane alternative.
  - The ramp never asks a wheel for more speed than it has.
  - Brake that can't hold the chair, and stops that time out still moving: the chair stays held and a new stop starts.
- **Faults:**
  - The fault paths (rail, silent VESC, lost remote, empty seat) all reach a safe state within 0.1 s.
  - Boot recovery and watchdog restart: the chair brakes if rolling and is held after a watchdog reset.
  - A silent VESC blind-brakes and keeps the chair held until DISARM is pressed.
- **Lean:**
  - Using only load a sensor has gained means an open sensor can't create motion.
  - Neutral trim is bounded at 15% of each range.
  - The reverse lock and the reverse-turn fade work as described.
  - Response exponents from the default knees are about 1.54 and 1.53, and the 1–4 bound is enforced.
- **Turning:**
  - Turning is smoothed on its own axis; release at 1800 deg/s² gives 7° of overshoot in the command, and the sideways-acceleration cap works.
  - The minimum-ERPM dead zone is real and correctly blamed. At the VESC default, a pivot does nothing below about 72% of full sideways lean, then jumps to about 110 deg/s (sim). Lowered to 3 RPM, it is proportional.
- **Buttons and modes:** ISR edges plus debounce, the Assist arm delay, DISARM working with an empty seat and during calibration, and the free-wheel toggle.
- **Settings and EEPROM:** CRC, commit byte and verify; calibration-only saves; profiles can't touch the chair's own fields; the watchdog is fed during saves; the bank and profiles fit in 4153 of the 4284 EEPROM bytes.
- **Edge cases:** millis rollover and divide-by-zero guards.
- **Tests:** the randomized rides with per-tick safety rules really test what they claim.
