The rule

Two limits, and the smaller one wins

Inside the VESC, power in equals power out (minus some heat). Power is volts × amps on each side:

motor volts × motor amps ≈ battery volts × battery amps

The battery side is pinned: about 53.5 V, and at most 3 A going in. The motor's voltage depends on speed: 6.6 V for every m/s. So the motor current the battery cap allows is:

motor amps allowed ≈ 3 A × 53.5 V ÷ (6.6 V/(m/s) × chair speed)

Units check: V ÷ (V/(m/s) × m/s) = V ÷ V, so the right side comes out in amps. In practice the VESC lets a bit more motor current through than this ideal number. The logs fit motor amps ≈ battery amps × 11.3 ÷ speed in m/s measured, and the chart below uses that fit.

The VESC firmware does exactly this, every control cycle. It sets the motor current target, then applies two limits, one after the other:

// VESC firmware 6.06, mcpwm_foc.c, "Apply current limits" (simplified)
// 1. battery limit, divided by duty (how fast the motor spins, as a fraction of full voltage)
iq_target = clamp(iq_target, l_in_current_min / duty, l_in_current_max / duty);
// 2. the motor-current setting
iq_target = clamp(iq_target, l_current_min, l_current_max);

So there are two ceilings on braking current. The battery ceiling is the curve: low at speed, rising as the chair slows, and going to infinity at a stop. The motor ceiling is a flat line, set in VESC Tool: −32.7 A on 11/22 and −20 A after the patch. Whichever is lower is what the VESC aims for. Drag the slider.

Fast end (right side)

The battery ceiling is low and it wins. At 4 m/s the 3 A cap allows only about 8 A of motor current, about 0.07 g of braking. The real court samples sit right on that curve. That's problem 2. Raising the battery cap lifts the whole curve.

Slow end (left side)

The battery ceiling shoots up, passes the motor ceiling, and stops mattering. Below about 1 m/s (today's settings) only the motor setting limits braking. Nothing here is wrong by itself. It becomes problem 1 when the firmware asks for maximum braking in this zone, and the current overshoots the target. That's the next four sections.
