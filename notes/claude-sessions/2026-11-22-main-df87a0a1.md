# Independent review: LEAN wheelchair control firmware (`3b24998`)

**Score: 7/10.** The firmware is careful and well tested, and nothing stops wheels-off bench testing. Three confirmed control defects should be fixed before anyone rides it: the chair rolls freely once a stop ends, a hard braking lean turns straight into full-speed reverse, and REMOTE skips the turn limits that LEAN has.

The full native test suite passes and `pio run` compiles for teensy41. I wrote my scenario simulations in `/tmp/lean-review-opus/` using the repo's own `Sim` test harness. Nothing in the repo was changed.

## 1. Findings, most severe first

### Real defects

**F1. After every stop the motors are released, and nothing brakes the chair again. On a slope it rolls away, and if the seat is empty it rolls away with nobody in it.** Confirmed by simulation.
- **Where:** `src/motion/StopController.h:121-122` ends the brake phase on the 1.5 s timeout or the 150 ms settle, then returns Release. `src/DriveController.cpp:275-276` sends zero current whenever no stop is running, and `:579` sends DISARM through that path.
- **What happens:** The rider gets out on a 1:12 ramp while in LEAN. The chair brakes to under 3 RPM, releases at about 0.8 s, and the empty chair then speeds up to 4.1 m/s after 8 s. Parked in DISARM on the same ramp, it reaches 2.8 m/s in 5 s.
- **Same gap in other states:** a VescSilent fault (blind brake for 1.5 s, then free), Assist, LEAN while not yet calibrated, and REMOTE waiting for a centred stick. All of these send `release()` whatever the wheels are doing.
- **Smallest fix:** in every state that isn't driving, send brake current whenever a fresh wheel speed is above about 3 RPM. Brake current can never drive. If feedback is gone, keep brake current on rather than timing out.
- **Trade-off for the team:** pushing the chair by hand in DISARM would be resisted. Assist exists for that, or add an explicit free-wheel condition. The default should not be a free roll.

**F2. A back lean to brake turns straight into reverse at the full speed-knob limit. Reverse has no cap of its own.** Confirmed by simulation.
- **Where:** `src/DriveController.cpp:529` (`command.speed * speedLimitMS_`, where speed can be -1) and `:549-552` for REMOTE.
- **What happens:** On knob 2 at 2.46 m/s, a full back lean held down stops the chair at about 0.85 s. Without pausing it accelerates backwards and reaches -2.46 m/s by 1.5 s. On knob 4 that becomes -4.9 m/s, backwards, with no rear view.
- **Why it matters:** "lean back to stop" is the panic gesture, and a startle reflex is to lean back and hold it.
- **Smallest fix:** cap reverse at a fraction of the knob limit (one constant, applied in both modes). Add a latch: once a back lean has brought forward travel to rest, hold zero until the lean comes back into the deadband before reverse is allowed.

**F3. REMOTE has no turn-rate or sideways-acceleration limit.** Confirmed by simulation.
- **Where:** `src/DriveController.cpp:544-553`. The `maxTurnRate` / `maxLateralAccel` cap at `:534-540` is applied only in LEAN.
- **What happens:** The remote's arcade mix with stick full forward and full sideways sends one wheel at 1 and the other at 0 (`controller-firmware/src/main.cpp:342-343`, sensitivity at 1.0). Pivoting from full speed:

| Speed knob | Peak yaw | Peak sideways accel |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 235 deg/s | 5.0 m/s² (your tipping estimate is 5.3) |
| 3 | 352 deg/s | 11.3 m/s² |
| 4 | 470 deg/s | 20.2 m/s² |

  The same manoeuvre in LEAN holds 2.5 m/s².
- **Smallest fix:** move the LEAN cap into a helper and clamp REMOTE's `turnTargetRPM` with it. About six lines.

**F4. The Assist pulse pattern depends on the control period.** Confirmed by reading.
- **Where:** `src/driveModes/PushAssist.h:223`, `elapsed % PulseMs >= PulseOnMs`, sampled once per control tick. Ticks land on exact multiples of `controlPeriod`, and the dashboard allows 2 to 15 ms.
- **What happens:** at 10 ms every sample has `elapsed % 10 == 0`, so the boost is on continuously, which doubles the designed average current. At 4 ms it is on about 60% of the time. Only 5 and 15 ms give the intended 50%.
- **Smallest fix:** toggle once per tick. If the pulsing isn't needed electrically, command the 50% average instead.

**F5. Assist counts a ramp steeper than about 1:10 as a push.** Confirmed by simulation, low severity.
- **Where:** `PushAccelMS2 = 1.0` at `src/driveModes/PushAssist.h:190`, which the comment justifies only for 1:12.
- **What happens:** on a 15% curb cut the motor adds downhill boosts of up to 22 A, about two and a half boosts in 5 s. It also matters because holding DISARM "to be sure" silently enters Assist.
- **Fix:** fold this into F1's brake-on-roll rule, or ignore pushes while the wheels are speeding up in the same direction they already roll.

**F6. A lost settings bank falls back to defaults without saying so.** Confirmed by reading, minor.
- **Where:** `src/config/Persistence.cpp:153-158`.
- **What happens:** calibration now saves itself, so EEPROM writes are more frequent. Losing power during one invalidates the single bank. The next boot runs on default zeros and ranges, with no log line and nothing in HEALTH.
- **Fix:** log a line at boot when `inspect()` fails, and add a flag to HEALTH.

### Matters of taste
- `knobs::AccelFraction` and `accelLimitMS2` (`src/drive_knobs.h:32,35`) are dead code. Acceleration is `MAX_ACCEL` on every knob position.
- `transitionPid` (`src/config/DriveSettings.cpp:432`) puts six dashboard fields on screen that do nothing. That works against complaint 2 ("too many knobs").
- `restingS_` (`src/DriveController.cpp:525`) carries over across a recalibration, so trim can start right after a fresh calibration. It's harmless.
- Neutral trim can ratchet: a slow slump at standstill moves neutral up to the 25% cap, and then sitting up straight reads as about a 9% forward command. The cap keeps this small, so it's a judgement call rather than a defect.

## 2. Checked and found sound
- **Sensor front end:** the FSRs feed an inverting transimpedance amp (MCP6004, 47k feedback, 0.3 V reference from the PCB copper). An open sensor reads about 372 counts, matching the measured value. A short saturates high and trips the 4000-count rail check. The "only gained load counts" rule means an open sensor removes a direction but can't create motion.
- **Pins and sensor roles:** pins, sensor roles and LED/button mapping match the PCB README.
- **Fault paths:** sensor rail (20 ms), VESC silent (100 ms), seat empty (100 ms) and remote timeout all go to a braking stop. Re-arming during a stop never resumes driving until there is valid input again (LEAN needs a new calibration, REMOTE needs a centred stick).
- **Stop controller:** the ramp starts no faster than the real wheel speed, stays within ±50 RPM of the measured speed, and hands over to brake current above the VESC minimum speed. That matches what the bldc source shows `mcpwm_foc.c` doing below min ERPM. One addition from that source: a speed command under min ERPM does not restart a motor that has been released, which the per-wheel brake fallback already covers.
- **Mode machine:** a stop press never becomes Assist, there is a 1 s lockout after a stop, DISARM always works (including during calibration and with an empty seat), Assist doesn't come back after the seat empties, and a bumped profile knob can be cancelled.
- **Boot recovery** after a watchdog reset brakes a rolling chair.
- **Arithmetic:** millis rollover on every timer, unit conversions, and the Spec B/P timing quoted in `drive_constants.h` (recomputed: 0.23, 0.56, 0.89, 1.22 s).
- **Config protocol:** writes are gated on DISARM and not stopping, edits roll back when validation fails, profiles keep the chair's calibration fields, the EEPROM layout fits (4153 of 4284 bytes), and the watchdog is fed during saves.
- **Tests:** the mode-machine and ride simulations are good. Not tested: the `main.cpp` glue (profile-knob pending logic, config gating, the mapping from command kind to CAN message, the dt cap), and each scenario behind F1 to F4.

## 3. Questions I couldn't settle
1. **False rail fault on a hard lean?** The amp crosses 4000 counts when the FSR resistance is at or below about 4.8 kΩ. If the heaviest rider's full forward lean gets there, SENSOR_RAIL stops the chair mid-ride. The logs can't answer this because run2 is clipped at 730 and the old live log is gain-scaled. Please log raw values at full lean on the bench.
2. **USB blocking:** on this Teensy core, can `Serial.write` block for about 120 ms when a host has the USB port open but isn't reading? That would be a control gap longer than Spec B allows.
3. **Freshness after a stall:** VESC frames are timestamped when processed, not when received, so buffered old frames count as fresh after a long stall. It seems harmless while stalls only happen in DISARM. Is that the intent?
4. **REMOTE and an empty seat:** REMOTE can't be selected with an empty seat but keeps driving if the seat empties. Is that the attendant use case you want?
5. **ERPM conversion:** has `VESC_ERPM_PER_WHEEL_RPM = 58.18` been checked on the chair? Every speed limit, the stop handoff and the sideways cap scale with it.

## 4. Score: 7/10
**What holds it down:** three confirmed behaviours a rider would meet in supervised riding. The chair free-rolls once a stop ends (including empty, on a slope), a braking lean flows into full-speed reverse, and REMOTE can command a pivot well past the tipping estimate. None of them affects wheels-off bench testing.

**What a 9 needs:** brake-on-roll in every non-driving state, a reverse cap with a brake-to-reverse latch, REMOTE going through the same turn and sideways limits, and the Assist pulse fix, each with a ride-simulation test. A boot notice for a lost settings bank would be a nice addition.
