Motor heat

Are we cooking the motors?

Not from what the logs show. The motor's rated current (10.8 A, from the design review) is a continuous rating: the current it can carry forever without overheating. Short bursts far above it are normal and expected. That's what the 32 A peak torque rating is for.

Heat in the coils goes as current squared, and a motor this size takes minutes to warm up. So what matters for heat is the average of current squared over minutes (the RMS), not the peaks. Over the 11/22 court sessions measured:

The 5-minute RMS stays around half the rated current. The worst single minute touches it. So the motors are fine for court testing. Three reasons not to relax:

  • A basketball game is harder than a test session: more starts, stops and turns, for 40+ minutes. Raising braking (fix 2) adds heat too. Log a real practice before trusting this.
  • The units may not match. The VESC reports current as the peak of each phase's sine wave. Motor datasheets usually rate current as RMS, which would make 10.8 A RMS about 15.3 A in VESC units, and we'd have even more margin. We need the DB87 datasheet to know which. estimate
  • Nothing watches motor temperature. Both VESCs are set up to read a 10 kΩ thermistor on the motor, but nothing is connected (they read −77 °C). If the DB87 has a temperature sensor wire, connecting it lets the VESC cut power before the motor overheats. Otherwise the Teensy can track the rolling RMS and warn.
