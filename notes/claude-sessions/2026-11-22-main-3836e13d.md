# Firmware review: branch at `b618750`, `wheelchair-firmware/src/`

**Score: 7/10.** The stop path, fault handling, calibration and test harness are careful and mostly right. Two things hold the score down: when the rider sits up, the wheels still coast below the VESC's minimum speed (so complaint #1, turn overshoot, isn't actually fixed on the default VESC setup), and Assist is in practice a cruise control that runs to the speed limit by itself.

**What I ran:** all native tests pass (12 suites) and `pio run` builds for teensy41. I wrote throwaway simulations in `/tmp/opus-lean-review/` that use the repo's own `Sim` wheel model. Nothing in the repo was changed and nothing was uploaded.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. After the rider sits up, the wheels coast below the VESC's minimum speed. Turns overshoot and the chair rolls on.
*Confirmed by simulation. High.*

Where: [DriveController.cpp:243](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/DriveController.cpp:243). Normal LEAN and REMOTE driving only ever sends RPM commands. `keepBraking` holds the command at 18 RPM only while the wheel is above 22 RPM. Below that, the command drops into the VESC's dead zone (under 15.5 RPM by default), the VESC lets go, and the wheel freewheels. Brake current is only used by the StopController, never in normal driving.

What the simulation shows:
- **Spin, then sit up (VESC default):** after a full-lean spin at 160 deg/s, the chair turned **248° more** and kept rotating for 4.5 s.
  - The comment at [drive_constants.h:67](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/drive_constants.h:67) promises about 14° from 160 deg/s.
  - With `s_pid_min_erpm` lowered to 3 RPM, it is still 35°.
- **Straight, then sit up:** from full forward, the wheels let go at about 17 RPM (0.5 m/s) and the chair rolled 2.2 m.
- **On a slope at neutral:** the chair freewheels until 22 RPM, gets braked back toward 18, and so creeps downhill at about 0.7 m/s.

The ride test that claims "after straightening up the rotation is gone" ([test:261](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/tests/native/drive_controller_test.cpp:261)) measures `yawDegS(sim)`. That is computed from the commands, not the wheels, so it can't catch this.

**Fix:** give each wheel its own command kind. When a wheel's command is inside the dead zone and the wheel is turning faster than that command (and above `STOP_DONE_RPM`), send brake current to that wheel. Add a test that checks the simulated wheels come to rest.

### 2. Assist runs the chair up to the speed limit by itself and is hard to get out of.
*Confirmed by simulation. High. This is the team's design, but as coded I think it is unsafe.*

Where: [PushAssist.h:31](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/driveModes/PushAssist.h:31). Boost depends on wheel speed alone, and the curve has exponent 0.6. That is positive feedback. The fade at 85% of the knob limit only caps where it settles.

In the test's wheel model (its 1.5 RPM/s per amp matches my estimate from the gearing):
- **Small pushes take off.** A 0.16 m/s nudge on knob 1 reaches the limit within 5 s. A 0.63 m/s push on knob 4 reaches **4.5 m/s in 20 s** with no further input.
- **Hand braking gets fought.** Slowing by hand at 0.5 m/s² still gets **8.5 A of forward boost** (mean). The slowdown detector ([PushAssist.h:52](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/driveModes/PushAssist.h:52)) only notices deceleration above about 1.3 m/s².
- **The ways out are weak or surprising:**
  - DISARM in Assist only releases the motors ([DriveController.cpp:138](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/DriveController.cpp:138) area), so the chair coasts at whatever speed Assist reached.
  - Assist turns itself back on when someone sits down, even though the LED was showing DISARM ([ModeStateMachine.cpp:116](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/ModeStateMachine.cpp:116)). During a transfer, the chair can push away from the person.
  - Pressing DISARM twice in a panic and holding the second press for half a second ends in Assist ([ModeStateMachine.cpp:90](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/ModeStateMachine.cpp:90)).

**Smallest fix:**
- Cap Assist at a low fixed speed, not the knob limit.
- Make DISARM brake above walking pace.
- Require a button press to re-enter Assist after the seat empties.
- Properly: drive the boost from a detected push (wheel acceleration) that decays over time, not from speed.

### 3. Any reset other than the watchdog, while rolling, lets the chair coast.
*Confirmed by simulation. Medium.*

Where: [main.cpp:188](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/main.cpp:188). The recovery brake only runs when `watchdogReset` is set. After a brown-out (battery sag under motor current) or any other reset at 3.8 m/s, the firmware's first commands are zero current. The VESCs would otherwise still be holding the last command, so the firmware actively releases them: in the sim the wheel was still at 116 RPM after 1 s.

**Fix:** always run the recovery path at boot. It already does nothing if the wheels report less than 3 RPM.

### 4. The startup mode can be LEAN, REMOTE or ASSIST.
*Confirmed by reading. Medium-low.*

Where: [DriveSettings.cpp:63](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/config/DriveSettings.cpp:63). Power-on in ASSIST with a rider seated boosts with no button pressed (and see #2). The test at line 971 checks that this is honoured.

**Fix:** only allow DISARM as the startup mode.

### 5. `VESC_STALE_MS = 200` misses Spec B's 0.1 s.
*Confirmed by reading. Low.*

Where: [drive_constants.h:59](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/drive_constants.h:59). The comment flags it. 100 ms is still 5 missed frames at 50 Hz, and CAN is essentially lossless, so I'd ship 100 ms as the default.

### 6. A cut during a save can wipe every setting.
*Confirmed by reading. Low; the defaults are safe.*

Where: [Persistence.cpp:116](<worktree>/wheelchair-firmware/src/config/Persistence.cpp). Settings are stored in a single bank, and the save writes the commit byte as 0 first. Saves now happen automatically after every tare and range calibration. A power-off during that window, or a watchdog reset during a worst-case flash erase, boots with every setting at default: "the feel changed with nothing changed" again.

**Fix:** two banks, A and B.

## Checked and sound
- **Stop path:** onset, ramp to handoff, brake current, settle. Measured or sent start speeds, ramp-overrun cap, blind stop when a VESC is silent.
- **Spec timings:** the Spec B per-knob times in the comments match my arithmetic (0.23 / 0.56 / 0.89 / 1.22 s). Spec P is met.
- **Unit conversions:** 58.18 ERPM per wheel RPM, 31.6 RPM per m/s, 11 mph.
- **Turn math:** turn-rate to half-difference conversion, lateral-acceleration cap, turn given priority within `MAX_RPM_COMMAND_DIFF`.
- **Faults:** checked whenever a mode is selected, even while the seat is empty; arming is refused while a fault persists.
- **Mode state machine:** DISARM works with an empty seat and during calibration. "A stop stays a stop" works. A bumped profile knob is cancelled by turning it back.
- **Calibration:**
  - Neutral is recaptured on every entry into LEAN, which fixes complaint #3 at its source.
  - "Gained load only" means an open sensor can't create motion.
  - Range-calibration acceptance is sensible.
  - Tare is refused or cancelled when the seat is occupied.
- **Remote:** recentre after 500 ms of silence, soft stop latches and needs a centred stick to clear.
- **Config protocol:** edits only in DISARM and not while stopping; profiles keep the calibration fields.
- **Watchdog:** register sequence and timeout steps.
- **Robustness:** millis rollover handling, dt capped at 4 periods, rail thresholds fit the MCP6004 rail-to-rail amplifier.
- **Telemetry:** 19 columns; whole lines are dropped rather than blocking.
