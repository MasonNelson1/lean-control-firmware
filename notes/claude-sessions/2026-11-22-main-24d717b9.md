# LEAN firmware audit (read-only)

I made no edits and no git writes. I ran the native tests on a copy in `/tmp/lean-audit/fw`, and all 9 suites pass. The probes and simulations I wrote also live in `/tmp/lean-audit` (`probe*.cpp`, `sim*.cpp`). They compile the real `LeanDriveMode`, `ModeStateMachine` and `RPMSetpointPID` sources.

**Hardware fact behind several findings.** Each force sensor feeds an MCP6004 amplifier, with the sensor between the inverting input and GND, a 47 kΩ feedback resistor, and a 0.3 V reference from Ra1/Rb1. The output is 0.3 V × (1 + 47k/R_fsr). That is roughly linear in force, and **an empty sensor reads ≈ 372 counts.** That 372 is where the "370 unloaded estimate" and `defaultFloor = 400` come from.

## 1. Findings, most severe first

**F1. The physical DISARM button does nothing when the seat is empty.** Confirmed by running it.
- Where: [ModeStateMachine.cpp:46-52](<repo>/wheelchair-firmware/src/ModeStateMachine.cpp:46). If any press is pending and `!in.seated`, every pending press is cleared, DISARM included.
- How it goes wrong: someone drives the empty chair in REMOTE and a bystander hits DISARM. Nothing happens; my probe printed `selected=REMOTE current=REMOTE`. Only the dashboard soft-stop or the Bluetooth timeout can stop it. The test at `mode_state_machine_test.cpp:57` locks this behaviour in.
- Fix: never apply the seat gate to `disarmPending_`.

**F2. One sensor fault drives the chair, since commit 7e90334 allowed negative readings.** Confirmed by probe.
- Where: [LeanDriveMode.cpp:14](<repo>/wheelchair-firmware/src/driveModes/LeanDriveMode.cpp:14), plus there is no range check on raw readings.
- An unplugged sensor reads like an empty one (372), far below its seated floor. Unplugged front sensor: speed **−0.77, reverse at 77% of the speed limit**. Unplugged back sensor: **+0.74 forward**.
- A shorted sensor drives the amplifier to the rail (4095), giving **full speed**. The 2-pin connector has GND right next to the signal, so a pinched cable is enough.
- The chair stays "seated" as long as the other three sensors carry the load.
- Fix: treat raw < ~340, raw > ~4000, or any one sensor sitting near 372 while seated as a fault, and go to DISARM. Before 7e90334 the clamp at 0 hid half of this.

**F3. Every stop path coasts or ramps, so the spec "wheel speed < 5% within 0.50 s" is not met.** Confirmed by reading and simulation.
- Seat loss, the DISARM button and calibration all send `vescSetCurrent(0)` ([main.cpp:379, 397, 435](<repo>/wheelchair-firmware/src/main.cpp:433)). Zero current means freewheel. If the rider falls out, the chair rolls on.
- A REMOTE timeout ramps down through the smoother instead. At speed knob 2 (2.46 m/s) it takes **0.1 s + 0.77 s and 1.1 m** to get under 5%; at knob 4 it takes 1.4 s and 3.8 m.
- The spec is physically incompatible with the firmware's own `MAX_ACCEL` of 3.7 m/s². From 2.46 m/s, stopping in 0.5 s needs about 5 m/s².
- Fix: add an emergency stop that commands RPM 0, or a brake current, at a separate, higher deceleration, then current 0 once stopped. Then decide whether the spec or the deceleration cap moves.

**F4. Nothing checks that the motor controllers are still talking, and there is no watchdog.** Plausible failure.
- `vescFresh()` exists ([vesc_can.h:126](<repo>/wheelchair-firmware/src/vesc_can.h:126)) but nothing calls it.
- **Wheel speed reports stop (status frames silent):** the measured speed freezes. LEAN and REMOTE then clamp every command to the frozen value ±50 RPM ([main.cpp:70-75](<repo>/wheelchair-firmware/src/main.cpp:70)). If it freezes at, say, 100 RPM, the lowest command it can send is 50 RPM, so **the chair cannot stop**. If one controller's status frames are never seen, that wheel is capped at 50 RPM while the other goes faster, giving an unexpected turn.
- **ASSIST:** keeps pushing current based on the frozen speed.
- **Teensy hangs:** there's no hardware watchdog, so the controllers keep the last command until their own timeout. The default in `pcb/vesc-reference/parameters_appconf.xml` is **1000 ms with 0 A brake current**.
- Fix: fault to DISARM if either controller has been silent for more than 100 ms. Enable the Teensy watchdog. In VESC Tool, set the app timeout to ≤ 100 ms with a nonzero brake current.

**F5. ASSIST has no speed cap and feeds back on itself.** No cap is confirmed by reading; whether it actually runs away depends on the motor constants.
- Where: [PushAssist.h:26](<repo>/wheelchair-firmware/src/driveModes/PushAssist.h:26). Boost = min(speed / max speed, 1)^0.6 × 25 A, so above the speed knob limit it keeps pushing the **full 25 A pulse**.
- With a 14.5:1 reduction, 12.5 A average is very likely more than rolling drag, so the chair keeps accelerating until the rider brakes by hand. Even then, the slowdown logic still delivers 30% boost against them.
- Fix: taper the boost to 0 at the speed limit and cut it above. Also cut it while the rider is braking, not just scale it.

**F6. The seat check mixes units and has no hysteresis.** Confirmed with the floors logged on the chair.
- Where: [LeanDriveMode.h:39](<repo>/wheelchair-firmware/src/driveModes/LeanDriveMode.h:39). It tests `2·Σ(raw−400) > W`, where W is the mean raw floor, offset included.
- With the floors from `device-monitor-261116-131125.log` (505/430/401/481), the rider counts as seated before calibration (W = 370) but **not seated after it** (W = 454). Pressing calibrate puts that rider into DISARM.
- W is not cleared by `reset()`, so the next rider is judged against the previous rider's threshold.
- A single 5 ms sample below the line drops to DISARM (coast), and re-entering LEAN clears the calibration, so a hard lean or a bump means recalibrating.
- Fix: seated = Σ(raw − 372) above a fixed threshold, independent of calibration. Use two thresholds: about 50 ms below to leave (still within the 0.1 s spec) and about 500 ms above to enter.

**F7. Dashboard gain edits do nothing until a reboot or profile switch.** Confirmed by reading.
- `applyRuntimeSettings()` ([main.cpp:97-111](<repo>/wheelchair-firmware/src/main.cpp:97)) never calls `resetDynamicGains()`.
- So Front/Rear/Left/Right gain edits have no visible effect. People keep turning them, save, reboot, and the chair suddenly feels very different.
- Fix: call `resetDynamicGains()` there, or better, remove per-sensor gains entirely (see section 2).

**F8. Dynamic-calibration gains live only in RAM.** Confirmed.
- They are lost on power cycle and wiped by `activateProfile()` ([main.cpp:155](<repo>/wheelchair-firmware/src/main.cpp:155)).
- A nudged profile knob also hijacks the LEAN button: pressing LEAN starts a profile confirm instead of entering LEAN. Holding it 2 s activates the profile, which **resets the gains**.

**F9. Wheel speed commands are rounded down to whole RPM.** Confirmed.
- Where: [vesc_can.h:78](<repo>/wheelchair-firmware/src/vesc_can.h:78). `vescSetWheelRPM(..., int32_t rpm)` truncates the float.
- Anything under 1 RPM becomes 0, and slow spin turns move in steps of 0.03 m/s that are always biased toward zero.
- Fix: make the parameter a `float`.

**F10. No bumpless restart and no anti-windup in the speed smoother.** Confirmed by reading.
- On re-entry the smoother restarts from 0 RPM even if the wheels are still rolling. For example: seat flicker, the chair coasts, the rider re-enters LEAN. The clamp then commands measured − 50 RPM, and the controller brakes as hard as it can.
- The smoother's internal speed is never pulled back to the clamped command. It can run ahead of the real wheel, which delays stops. The TODO at [main.cpp:27](<repo>/wheelchair-firmware/src/main.cpp:27) already admits this.
- Fix: seed the smoother from the measured speed on entry, and write the clamped value back into its state.

**F11. The calibration button is accepted at any time.** Plausible failure.
- It works while moving or while leaning, and the neutral floors are sampled about 50 ms after the press, while the rider's arm is still reaching for the button.
- Neutral then shifts, and sitting upright becomes a lean command. A deliberate press longer than about 300 ms starts dynamic calibration, gets rejected, and DISARMs.
- Fix: only accept calibration when the wheels are stopped. Sample neutral for about 1 s starting about 0.5 s *after* release.

**F12. Config limits allow values outside the spec.**
- Controller timeout can be set as high as 500 ms ([DriveSettings.cpp:107](<repo>/wheelchair-firmware/src/config/DriveSettings.cpp:107)); even the default 100 ms plus one 5 ms tick is 105 ms, just over 0.10 s.
- Button debounce up to 150 ms ([:109](<repo>/wheelchair-firmware/src/config/DriveSettings.cpp:109)) delays the DISARM button past 0.10 s.
- Left and right controller IDs can be swapped from the dashboard, which reverses turning.
- Remote drive packets carry no CRC, unlike config packets.

**Check in VESC Tool (not visible in this repo):**
- `s_pid_min_erpm`: the VESC default of 900 ERPM is **15.5 wheel RPM, about 0.49 m/s**. Below that the VESC speed loop doesn't regulate. A full standstill turn lean at knob 2 asks for only 26 RPM, and a half lean for 9 RPM.
- If that default is active, it alone explains "nothing, then suddenly" on turns, and coasting through the stop.

## 2. The three complaints

**Turning overshoot**
- *Delay after the rider straightens up.* The smoother needs **0.36 s and about 0.17 m of wheel travel** to stop a full standstill turn at knob 2. With a 0.6 m track width (my assumption), that's **about 32° of extra rotation**, plus a 2 RPM reverse dip.
- *A floor on that delay.* At the 117 RPM/s acceleration cap, about 16° is the minimum. On top of that come the controller's own speed loop, the possible minimum-ERPM dead zone, and the rider's reaction time. Hitting 90° by eye at about 160°/s cannot work.
- *Turn rate keeps changing:*
  - it scales with the speed knob (4× from knob 1 to knob 4);
  - it scales with the turn-mix gain, which is driven by the *commanded* speed rather than the real speed (3× from standstill to full);
  - it's asymmetric: the sensor on the unloading side bottoms out at 372, so full left vs full right gave turn −0.385 vs +0.225 in my probe;
  - it's coupled to forward/back lean whenever the left and right gains differ (`run2/DIRECTION_ANALYSIS.md` already shows 0.27 of turn during a straight backward lean).
- **What I'd change:**
  - make sideways lean command a turn rate in deg/s, independent of the speed knob;
  - cap the standstill turn rate at about 60–90°/s;
  - give the turn component its own short, unthrottled path back to zero;
  - add a gyro and close the loop on turn rate. That is the real fix for hitting an angle.
  - The deliberate −0.3 reverse threshold also flips the turn direction in one step. Blend it over a short band instead of switching.

**"Nothing, then suddenly everything"**
- The 0.05 deadband and the 1.54 exponent come first, then per-sensor gains of 2/3/1/1.
- Below that, whole-RPM truncation and the likely 15.5 RPM controller dead zone mean small commands produce nothing. Once a command crosses the threshold the controller suddenly regulates.
- "Weight normalization" isn't normalizing by weight: W ≈ 372 + average load, so it's about 80% amplifier offset. The gains end up doing all the work, which is why there are so many knobs.
- **What I'd change:** one calibrated range per axis and direction, one curve per axis, and delete per-sensor gains and W.

**Feels different after reseating**
- Neutral is sampled at the moment of the button press (F11), and right after sitting down, while the cushion is settling and the sensors creep upward.
- Unequal gains turn any change that hits all four sensors equally into a phantom lean.
- Turn authority depends on which side sensor sits near empty (see turning above).
- The seat threshold carries over from the previous calibration (F6), and seat flicker forces recalibration.
- Dynamic gains vanish on power cycle or profile switch (F8), and dashboard gain edits apply late (F7).
- **Biggest single fix:** compute lean as the **centre of pressure**. Subtract 372 from each sensor, then take x = (r−l)/Σ and y = (f−b)/Σ. Dividing by the live total cancels rider weight, creep, and how hard the rider sits. Re-zero over 1 s after release, and require the wheels to be stopped.

**Why dynamic calibration "worked and then didn't"**
- Its gains are lost on reboot or profile change (F8).
- Calibrating can itself make a lighter rider read as unseated (F6).
- A slightly long press goes into dynamic calibration, gets rejected, and DISARMs (F11).
- When the gains do apply, standstill turn authority roughly triples compared with the defaults.

## 3. Checked and sound

- Mode state machine: profile confirm, the assist hold, and calibration discarding button presses.
- Button interrupt to debounce handoff.
- Speed knob decode: classification, fault falls back to slowest, the speed cap comes down gradually.
- Config writes are allowed only in DISARM.
- Config protocol: CRC, rollback on failed cross-field checks, validation of uploaded profiles.
- EEPROM storage: commit byte with read-back verification.
- Telemetry: drops whole Bluetooth lines instead of blocking.
- REMOTE: timeout and soft-stop latch, reset on mode entry.
- Seat-loss *detection* latency of ≤ 5 ms in LEAN and ASSIST.
- Smoother guards against invalid inputs.
- CAN frame encoding and decoding, and the turn-mix interpolation.

The config framework and tests are well built; the control law and the safety layer are not.

## 4. Score: 5/10

The infrastructure is solid. The rest:
- F1, F2 and F4 are real ways to get unexpected motion or no stop.
- None of the stop paths meets the 0.5 s wheel-speed spec.
- The lean-to-wheel math is the main cause of the turning and consistency complaints.

My order: F1 → F2 → F4 → F3 → F6, then the centre-of-pressure and turn-rate rewrite.
